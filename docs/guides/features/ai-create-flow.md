## AI create & build flow (`components/create`, `dashboard/workspace-prompt`)
- **Dashboard home speaks the design's own words** (Figma Ai-Flow 00/01, `dashboard/workspace-prompt` + `dashboard/sidebar`) — "What will you build today?" over the Figma subtitle and a soft `--gradient-ai` wash, **Generate with AI · Build manually** as a plain toggle, and a composer carrying `useVoiceInput` dictation + **Enhance prompt** — **no attach button**: it was permanently disabled (the picked `File` was never sent to the generator), a control that offered nothing but its reason, and it comes back when an image can go with the prompt; **Enter sends and Shift+Enter breaks the line**, as the chat and the Refine box always did (this box alone wanted ⌘/Ctrl+Enter); its send has two real states (a quiet disabled ai-magic square with nothing typed, the primary **Generate** once there is a draft). Under the composer, **You'll get** lists the five pieces a build really delivers — *3D model · PCB · Firmware code · Wiring · Parts*, from `ITEM_KINDS`/`ITEM_LABELS` — where it used to promise *Schematic · Parts list · Build steps*. Example ideas are a pool of nine with three on screen and a **Shuffle** that really rotates the window. **Get inspired** cards read the same feed dataset the Innovations grid reads, so each links to a project that exists and carries its minted badge and real counts; the card's border answers a hover (the picture no longer zooms as well). The sidebar names the **Builder plan** as *not on sale yet* rather than offering an Upgrade button with nothing behind it; **Explore marketplace · Messages · Blog** have no page yet, so each is an `aria-disabled` row reading **Soon** instead of a link to a 404; a Support block (each row disabled with the reason until it has a destination) and, beside the account row, a wallet → `/history#credits` and a bell listing the builds needing attention (its count sits on the error ink, 4.83:1 — white on the error fill was 3.76:1 at 10 px) — minus the build whose chat is open, which is already in front of the maker (`useAttentionElsewhere`), and each row opens that build's chat. **The keyboard reaches it in order**: *Skip to main content* is the first Tab stop on every dashboard and create page (it lives in both shells now, before the sidebar — it sat sixteen stops in, after the whole nav, where skipping was pointless); the *Generate with AI · Build manually* radio group is one Tab stop with the arrows moving the choice (both options were stops of their own); and the prompt card shows focus in the brand border the chat composer wears (it went from `border` to `border`, which changed nothing).
- Concept chat → **build job** with live status (`build-shell`, `build-status`, `concept-chat`, `chat-thread`, `confirm-build-dialog`) → **five deliverables**: 3D · PCB · Firmware code · Wiring · Parts.
- **The build runs inside the chat, not on a page of its own** (`concept-chat` + `chat-thread` + `build-rail` + `review-outputs`) — confirming the gate used to `router.push('/build/<id>')`, a separate full-screen surface with a “← Back” link, so the conversation, the rail and the composer all vanished at the moment the work got interesting. The job is **derived from the chat** (newest `BuildJob` whose `chatId` matches, so a reload lands back on it rather than on an empty canvas) and simply takes the canvas over: the **build's pipeline merges into the rail's product rows** the moment the build starts — every row keeps its fixed `n of 5` from the first second and gains a live progress bar, with the five-piece breakdown open under every row while the build has one or two products and under the selected row alone once it has three or more — and the canvas becomes `ReviewOutputs` running live, one tab per product over one tab per deliverable, each panel reading *Generating · N%* until it lands. The composer stays where it is throughout. A **queued** build is the same surface (every row reads *Waiting*); what it is waiting for, and its **Cancel**, sit in the rail's states card (the separate queued notice above the composer is gone — two places said it). **The 3D enclosure is generated here** (`use-build-model.ts`, shared with the `/build` page): it used to run on that page alone, so a build reviewed in its chat never started a model and the 3D tab spun for ever; a failed or timed-out (6 min) generation is now recorded on the job (`BuildJob.modelFailed`) and the 3D tab's shell says *The concept shape couldn't be made* with **Try again**. **`/build/<id>` sends anyone whose browser holds the chat straight to it** (`router.replace`, keeping `?tab=`), so there is one surface per build; the page itself remains only for a job whose chat is gone, where Back goes to History. The ready/retry toast and the bell follow: they open `/chat/<chatId>`.
- **The build page is two panes, like the concept surface** (`build-shell.tsx` + `create/build-rail.tsx`) — it used to BE the pipeline: five rows per product down the middle of the screen, with nothing to look at until the last piece finished. That is the wrong way round. The list of what will be made is small and fixed; the thing worth the screen is what has been made. The **rail** states the whole pipeline from the moment the build starts, every piece including the ones that have not begun (a list that grows as it goes says nothing about how much is left), grouped under each product because the same five pieces run once per product and *PCB* alone would not say whose. The **canvas** is the review surface, running **live**: it opens with the build rather than after it, its product tabs and output tabs are there throughout, and a piece that has not landed says what it is doing in its own panel instead of showing an empty frame. The decisions at the foot — Save Project, Open in editor — wait for the whole build, because they act on all of it. The whole-build states (queued, waiting on credits, the overrun stop, the partial and system failures with their retries) stay on the card written for them, now `statesOnly` in the rail: two lists of the same five rows side by side is one list too many.
- **The build page is one card driven by one state table** (`build-shell.tsx` + `build-status.tsx`, Ai-Flow 11–14) — a plain “← Back” link (back where you came from, else the source chat) over one 580 px card: the locked-concept strip (`CONCEPT n · IN BUILD` chip · title · spec line · state badge), a banner, the five artifact rows (icon · label · `ITEM_SUBTITLES` · status — *Waiting · NN% · Ready · Couldn't generate · Didn't run*, with a progress bar only while a piece is building or finished), the clock line, and a footer that ends in one link. Every string comes from `stateRowFor(job, now)`, so **queued · waiting on credits · running · partial · system failure** can't be described one way in the badge and another in the footer: a partial build names the steps that failed and the ones that are safe (`The PCB step failed` · `The PCB and Wiring steps failed`) and offers the per-row **Retry <piece>**, while a system failure says nothing ran and carries the whole-build **Try this build again** (which really restarts and re-charges the build) beside **Back to chat** — and when a piece had already landed, the failure copy names it (*only your 3D model was finished*) instead of claiming nothing was delivered. Verified in both themes over CDP (210 checks) plus a 38-case unit check of the table.
- **A queued build can be cancelled; a running one that overruns can be stopped** (Part 4 §4.6) — two different answers because the money is in two different places. **Cancel** is offered on a queued build only (`cancelBuild` in `history.tsx`, a no-op on any other status, guarded inside the updater so a status that changed between the click and the call still decides it). It promises no refund, because a queued build has never been charged: the simulator charges when a build *starts*, and the line under the control says exactly that. **Stop and refund** appears on a running build once `isOverrunning(job, now)` — past roughly twice its estimate, the spec's own threshold — and here a refund really is owed, so it marks the job a system failure and lets the simulator's existing refund effect return the credits rather than opening a second path to the ledger. The offer rides the card's own `useMinuteClock`, so no new timer. Both live in the chat now: the rail carries `BuildStatus statesOnly inChat` under the pipeline whenever the build is not ready (queued with its Cancel, paused on credits, partial or system failure with the retry, the overrun stop), so none of it depends on the `/build` page any more. Verified over CDP: cancel removes the queued job, leaves the balance alone and returns to the chat, while a running build offers no cancel; and a job 25 minutes into a 10-minute estimate shows the offer, where one at 12 minutes does not, with the stop returning 4 credits.
- **The review card is headed by the project, and carries what the project could become next** (`review-outputs.tsx`) — the heading used to read *Review your deliverables*, which describes the surface the maker is already looking at: the tabs, the panels and the footer all say that, while the one thing the card could not tell you was **which project this is**. The eyebrow carries the state (`BUILD READY` · `BUILDING`) and the heading carries the subject — the project the maker named at the question, else the project this build was saved into, else the primary product's own title. Beside it, right-aligned on the same row and flush with the footer's own controls, sits the next step a finished project can take: **Create Mobile App**, a tier below *Save Project* — the decision this card exists to take — in the quiet outline the card already uses for *Open in editor*, one size down (36 px). **Add Network is no longer here** (owner decision 2026-09-30): the network is worked out at the concept stage, in the rail's Network section, and becomes the project's network on Save. *Honest limit: Create Mobile App has no engine behind it yet, so it is greyed and says so in its tooltip (`Create Mobile App isn't built yet`) rather than accepting a press and doing nothing.* **The header holds its shape**: a small state word over the project name (no caps eyebrow), and the next step stays beside it at any width — opening the issue list used to widen the left column and push it onto a row of its own. Create Mobile App says **Soon** on the control itself and is `aria-disabled` but focusable, so the reason reaches a keyboard and a touch screen, not only a mouse. **No card inside the card**: the "what this covers" column is divided from the artifact by a hairline instead of a bordered box, its heading is plain *What this covers*, and its lines are a list — the check-mark pills read as "verified" and wrapped inside themselves. The product switcher and the deliverable tabs are one tab language on two levels (neutral fill for the product, the brand's quiet tint for the deliverable, a hairline between). **Both tab lists keep the tab pattern they announce**: one Tab stop each (the selected tab), the arrows move the selection with focus following, Home and End jump to the ends, and the product tabs point at the panel they drive (`aria-controls`) — every tab was its own Tab stop and the arrows did nothing.
- **"Review your deliverables" is where a finished build lands** (`create/review-outputs.tsx`, Ai-Flow 15 · DL-02 · DL-03 · Wiring · Parts) — the `BUILD READY` card carries **one pill tab per artifact the build really made** (3D model · PCB · Firmware code · Wiring · Parts, in `ITEM_LABELS` order; `?tab=<kind>` preselects one, so another surface can link straight at the parts list), the artifact itself on the left inside a 520 px scroll panel (the previews derived from the build's own parts — the 3D tab is the model panel, below) and a **WHAT THIS COVERS** card beside it reading `WHAT_SHIPS[kind]` — it describes what the build covers, since the surface offers no download and the old heading named file formats (Gerber bundle · STL + STEP · BOM CSV) nothing here hands over —, with `PartsSummary` above it on the Parts tab. Its footer is the only decision, and **Save Project is one click**: `projectFromBuild` makes the project the maker already chose at the setup question — an existing one (this build's products are added to what it holds; its headline stays its own) or a new one under the name they typed — with **every product and the description the model wrote for it** in `ManualProject.products`, and `setBuildProject` records it. It used to open the Brief and ask *What's your idea?* and *sell, give or keep private?* before anything was saved at all. **Open in editor** makes (or reuses) the same project and opens it at `/project/<slug>/pcb`. **One project per build**: `projectFromBuild` hands the same one back on a second press, a second visit, or two presses inside one tick (a by-build ref, since React state hasn't flushed yet inside that tick), and it never switches the active project — only opening the editor does. **Once the build carries a project the footer says so** — *Saved to `<name>` as version {n} of its chat, once it has more than one — the name a link to `/projects/<id>`.* over **Add Brief** (`/build/<id>/brief`, which then runs on the project's own draft and opens on its products) and **Open in editor** (COR-40); a stored `projectId` whose project is gone reads as unsaved (verified: one click → *Saved to Drone B1*, the project holding both products with their descriptions). **A failed piece is retried from its own panel** — *Retry PCB*, which costs nothing — instead of a sentence pointing at a page the chat no longer sends anyone to; a system failure's panel says the piece didn't run and where the whole-build retry is. **The card opens on something to look at**: a finished piece — the 3D tab as soon as its piece is, since the model is built from the parts and the concept mesh only replaces its shell. The active deliverable tab is the brand's quiet tint, not a second solid violet beside Save Project. The footer counts every product's pieces (*All 10 pieces are ready*), not a fixed five. A build with nothing to review keeps the teaching empty state instead of empty tabs. **Saving is what ends the review** — `buildAttention` reads a build's `projectId` as "already taken somewhere", so the sidebar bell and the ready toast go quiet the moment Save Project or Open in editor makes the project; the review reason used to clear only on an `outcome`, which nothing sets since the picker was removed, so a saved build asked to be reviewed forever (verified: bell `1 needs attention` → `No notifications yet`, still quiet after a reload). *(Removed with the redesign: the Private/Community/Sell outcome picker and its dialog, the per-kind "What is this?" blurb and the Download .glb link — minting and outcomes are the Brief module's step; `/api/projects` is untouched, and the store's `setBuildOutcome` / `BuildOutcome` went with the picker, since nothing set an outcome any more.)* Also fixed here: a build-status row that reached **Ready** before a later system failure keeps its success tone — greying it denied work that really was produced. Nothing here promises what the card cannot hand over: the PCB caption dropped *Gerber + KiCad* (there is no download on this surface) and the Parts row reads *Bill of materials, grouped by function* rather than *with suppliers*, a column the table does not have. A companion is drawn from *"Charger for a handheld soil meter…"* — the parent's sentence joined as a phrase, not *"for A handheld…"*.
- **The 3D tab is an assembly you can take apart** (`create/model-panel/*` + `lib/three/assembly.ts`, Figma Creator Panel V3.0 *3D Module* `47167:21997`, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-25-3d-model-review-design.md`) — it used to be one `.glb` mesh, and with no Meshy key every build's enclosure was the same sample duck beside copy saying it was sized to the board. The model is now **composed from the build's own data** (`deriveAssembly`): the product's parts with the spec sheet's real body sizes (a booked build's pack sized by the spec's own table), on a board at the spec's size (none for a product without one), in an enclosure — a base with four board bosses and a lid, the spec's material and wall — whose shell is the concept mesh when Meshy made one and **never the demo sample**. A build older than the spec is sized from its parts the same way. Designators and quantities are the **BOM's own** (`bomFor`), so a part reads the same here as on the Parts tab. Systems come from the part categories (Enclosure · Board · Compute & radio · Sensing · Motion · Power · Interface · Passives — only the non-empty ones), and Material / Mass appear **only where they are known** (the shell by volume × density, the board as FR-4); a part matched by its category default says *estimate*. The panel is a 522 px viewer beside a 282 px rail. The viewer carries a toolbar (Home · Fit · zoom · Fullscreen), an explode card (0–80 % outward, then onto an inventory grid reached at 100 %, with A11's value bubble while dragging) and the design's hint line for each state; hovering rings the part and names it (`<name> · <ref>`), a click opens its detail in the rail (Part reference · Quantity *n of N* · Material · Mass · System, **Isolate this part**, the other parts in its system as buttons), isolation shows it alone with *Isolated · 1 of N parts*, each system has a switch with live counts (*6 of 9* in brand), all off shows *Nothing to show*, and fullscreen is the browser's own (a fixed overlay where it is refused) with 3/4 · Front · Side · Top. Esc walks isolated → selected → fullscreen; the viewer takes focus, ←/→ step through the visible parts and Enter picks one, announced in a live region. Loading shows the design's card over a skeleton rail with a bar that follows the real stages (the viewer's chunk, then the first frame); a lost WebGL context shows *We couldn't open the 3D model* with **Try again**; at phone width the rail stacks under the viewer. Each product tab gets its own assembly. *Honest limits: the parts are sized boxes, cans and chips — nothing is modelled in detail — and the tooltip's and value bubble's shadows are left out because the design system has no effect style for them. Deviations from the frames: the isolated badge sits beside the toolbar (the frame puts it under it), the presets use the DS `ButtonGroup`'s quiet selected state (the frame draws a solid one), and the copy that named a STEP assembly and STL/STEP files says what the build really has.* Verified over CDP with SwiftShader: a 24-check matrix in both themes, the stacked layout at 400 px, a companion's own assembly, forced context loss and recovery, and measured against the frames (toolbar at 16,16 with 40 × 36 buttons, explode card 56 px and 30 px from the foot, rail 282 × 522 at 14 px).
- **The ready/retry/credits toast is top-centre, one shape, three tones** (`create/attention-banner.tsx`, Ai-Flow 20) — a `role="status"` card fixed `top-[16px]`, centred (`left-1/2 -translate-x-1/2`, 540 px), reading `topAttention` off the same history store the sidebar bell and build page read: **review** (brand icon tile, `BUILD READY TO REVIEW`, `<title> is ready`, **Open build**) · **retry** (warning tile, `BUILD NEEDS A RETRY`, `<title> · the <failed steps> step failed`, **Open build**) · **credits** (warning tile, `BUILD PAUSED`, `<title> is paused — top up credits to start it`, **Top up credits** → `/history#credits`) — the credits branch used to fall through into the review styling with no copy of its own. The enter transition is 150–250 ms and static under `prefers-reduced-motion`; × calls `dismissAttention` (the sidebar's red dot stays on independently). Auto-hides on the build's own page. Its state reads in sentence case (*Build ready to review* — no caps eyebrow) and names the work by its project (`projectChoiceName`, else the title). **The video render toast speaks the same language** (`video-jobs/global-render-indicator.tsx`): it was an inverted dark strip with uppercase coloured *CANCEL / REVIEW* links; it is now the same surface card — a tinted state tile, *Video rendering · about N min left* over the title, one outlined action (*Review · Try again · Cancel*) and a ×. It also lives in its **own bottom-right slot** (`ideeza-toast-layer-render` in `app/layout.tsx`): a render reports for minutes, and top-centre it sat over every page's heading and Back link for all of them. **At phone width the attention toast docks at the foot** — top-centre it covered the menu button and the Canvas/Chat tabs, so nothing could be reached until it was dismissed — and stands on top of a render toast when one is up (`body:has(.ix-render-toast)`), so the two never overlap (verified at 400 and 1700 px).
- **A product is named, not quoted back** (`deriveTitle` in `lib/create/concept.ts`) — when the summarizer is unreachable the deterministic fallback used to truncate the maker's whole sentence at 56 characters, so *“A camera drone with a handheld remote controller and a c…”* became the heading of a card, a product tab and a rail group, three times on one screen. It reads the head of the phrase instead: the leading request and article come off (*build me a…*, *I want to make an…*), the phrase stops at the first word that starts a second clause (`with · and · that · for · using …`), and the rest is title-cased with the maker's own acronyms left alone — *Camera Drone*, *Smart Plant Watering System*, *NFC Crypto Tap Card*, *LED Matrix Clock*. It invents nothing; it stops where the sentence stops being a name, and falls back to the old truncation when the head yields nothing usable. A model title over 40 characters is shortened the same way (`parseConcept`), where it used to be cut mid-word — *Bike Tail Light with Automatic Brighteni* on the card, the rail and the composer.
- **A prompt can describe a system, not just an object** (Part 4 §4.4, `lib/create/companions.ts` + `api/concept/companions` + `create/companion-picker.tsx`) — accepting a concept asks whether this product needs other *products* to work (a drone needs a remote, an earbud a charging case), and the answer fills a **Products in this build** section that **leads** the Generate gate — it decides how many deliverables there are (five per product), so it reads before the manifest that lists them. One dialog, not a screen in front of another one asking the user to approve the same build twice. The concept the chat started from is the **first row** rather than a separate card above it, so there is one list and one place to look. The gate **reads that decision rather than retaking it**: it used to classify a second time when *Use this concept* was pressed, which asked the model the same question again, got a different list back, and threw the maker's own answer away. Classification is automatic, and it answers in two steps. The model first, since only it can reason about a product nothing has heard of; when it cannot answer, a **deterministic rule** does (`classifyByRule` in `lib/create/companions.ts`) — a small hand-written table of the families where a companion really is a separate product: a drone is flown with a controller, earbuds live in a case, a sensor node reports to a base station. The rule exists because the text provider stopped serving and every build then took the silent path, so the feature read as *"your product needs nothing"* when in truth nothing had run — silence that looks like an answer is its own kind of lie. It **cannot invent**: every companion it offers is written in that table by hand, and anything outside it is a single product, which remains the honest default and the common case. Verified against nine prompts: the drone, earbuds, sensor node, smart lock, fitness band and robot vacuum are systems; the bedside lamp, kitchen timer and bicycle speedometer are not. *(Plurals are spelled out on every head noun — `\bearbud\b` does not match "earbuds", and a missing `s?` silently drops a whole family with no error anywhere.)* **AI Horde text was measured and rejected for this**: its JSON was perfect (6/6, ~7 s) but its judgement was not — every one of six prompts came back `isSystem: true`, including a kitchen timer needing a "Remote control" and a speedometer needing "Speedometer, Speedometer, Speedometer". Most concepts are one object and never see the screen (verified: a drone returns a remote and a charger, a desk lamp returns nothing). The list is **AI-generated and read-only**; the original concept is always included and has no control beside it; selection blocks nothing. A ticked row's **Generate concept** runs **in the dialog**: it stays open, the row shows the render's own progress as a percentage and a bar, and the preview lands in place. Every row carries a small preview, and clicking one opens it larger inside the row with **Refine** (describe a change) and **Regenerate** beside it — §4.4.5's "behaves exactly like the primary concept loop", without sending the user back to the thread and asking them to return. The prompt inherits the parent's own words, so the products read as a family. The primary's row is preview-only: it was approved by pressing Use this concept, and its own refine and regenerate live on its card in the chat. The **estimate is live** and counts only what still has to be rendered, because §4.8 says deselecting keeps the concept — re-selecting must not charge twice. **§4.4.3's escape hatch is real**: the gate always carries a field to name a product the classifier did not offer (*Single-product build — name a companion product…* when it found none), and the row it adds is plainly the user's own, so a classifier miss is never a dead end. The cost chip follows the selection — `Uses 4 credits` becomes `Uses ~5 credits` the moment a companion needing a concept is ticked.
- **"Use this concept" says it is working** — pressing it runs two model round-trips (read the concept back, then classify it) before the gate can open, which is seconds of nothing. The button becomes a disabled, `aria-busy` **Preparing…** with a spinner for exactly that window, on the card that was pressed.
- **A build carries every product, not just the first** (`BuildJob.companions`, `productsOf`, `allItems`) — §4.4.8 puts all products in one project, so they are one job. Each product has its own five artifacts, its own concept and its own parts, and every rollup reads the whole job: a build whose drone is finished while its remote still renders is **running**, not ready, and the shell keeps showing the status card until all of them land. The build page groups the rows under each product's name, the review surface grows a **product switcher**, and each product's deliverables are derived from its own parts (verified: the drone's BOM lists Drone MCU, the remote's lists Remote MCU). A per-artifact retry, a whole-build retry and a system failure all address the right product. Failure copy names the product too, since "the PCB step failed" would not say whose.
- **Every product carries a confidence badge** (§4.3, `lib/create/confidence.ts` + `create/confidence-badge.tsx`) — output is **always delivered**; the badge says what checking it passed. Two tiers, `Checked` and `Draft`; **`Verified` is deliberately not rendered**, because the spec says an unreachable top tier only tells users something is being withheld. A `Draft` badge opens the grouped issue list (§4.3.5: design rule · assembly · works-with), and each row says whether a check **ran and found something** or **could not run at all** — different things, so they do not read the same. **Today every product is `Draft`**, and honestly so: a build produces a parts list, a net graph and board dimensions but no copper, so `runDrc` has no geometry to measure and the row says exactly that instead of implying a fault the user could fix. What *is* really checked is **§4.4.10's cross-product compatibility**, which needs no geometry — radio protocol and charging connector are read from each product's parts, a mismatch is reported in plain words, and it lands in **both** products' lists. The project's headline state is the lowest tier of any product (§4.4.9). The `Draft` refund sentence (§4.3.4) sits inside the list, where someone reading their issues will see it. **A check that could not run is not a fault**: when every issue on a product is *not run* — the case on every single-product build today — the `Draft` badge is neutral (`bg-bg-subtle`, an info glyph), reads *N checks not run yet*, and the list opens on *Not checked yet — … nothing has been verified. Nothing was found wrong either* (`DRAFT_UNCHECKED_MEANING`). It used to be amber and headed *Design rule issues found* over rows that said NOT RUN, which made the end of every build read as a problem. Amber is kept for a check that ran and found something (the works-with mismatch), where the count reads *2 to review · 2 not run* and the sentence says *Issues found — review them before you manufacture* — true of every group, which "design rule issues" was not.
- **The balance a control reads is the balance you have** — `canAfford` answered from a ref that an effect advanced, and an effect runs *after* the render that read it, so on the render right after hydration every caller still saw the pre-hydration balance of 0 and nothing re-rendered afterwards to correct it: a chat holding 396 credits sat there reading *“You are out of credits”* with the composer, Refine, Regenerate and the gate all greyed out. It reads `state.balance` now, which is what render-time callers need; the ref stays for `charge`/`refund`, which decide at event time and have to see their own charges inside one tick.
- **Credits are a real ledger** (`lib/create/credits.tsx` + `create/credits-card.tsx`) — a build costs `BUILD_COST` (4) credits **per product** (`buildCost(products)`) and every concept render costs `CONCEPT_COST` (1) — the first draft, a refine and a regenerate alike. The per-product price is what the canvas and the gate quote **and what the worker charges**: `build-simulator` used to charge a flat `BUILD_COST` and check affordability against it, so a two-product build said 8 credits and took 4 (verified now: −8 for two products, balance 398 → 390, the same figure the gate showed as `398 → 390 after`). A render is charged inside `runGeneration`, the single funnel every render goes through, keyed by the turn it will land in; a render that never arrives is **refunded**. Every control that starts one — the home composer's send, the chat composer's send, Refine, Regenerate and a failed card's *Try again* — is greyed with the reason when the balance cannot cover it. Charge and refund are keyed and idempotent, the balance and every entry persist to `localStorage`, and the Credits card lists the ledger in words, naming each build by its title (`describeEntry(entry, subject)`, never the raw id) under copy that matches it (*per product*, and a render costs 1 — it used to claim refining was free above −1 lines). There is no payment integration, so the free top-up says so plainly.
- **The queue cannot wedge, and the worker is held by work, not by a flag** (`isWorkingBuild` + `startPendingItems` in `lib/create/history.tsx`) — `promoteQueued` started `job.items` alone, so a promoted **multi-product** build began with the primary's five artifacts building and every companion's rows left `pending` for ever. The tick only advances what is `building`, so the primary finished, the companions never moved, the job never derived as done — and it held the single worker permanently: every build booked after it came back *queued* and sat on **Waiting** with nothing behind it, for good. Promotion now starts every product's waiting rows. Two things stop it recurring: "is the worker busy" is `isWorkingBuild` — stored `running` **and** something actually in flight — rather than the stored flag alone, which nine call sites trusted and which no longer means anything once a job is finished; and a job found holding the worker with nothing in flight is **picked up where it stopped** rather than left there, so a build already wedged in someone's browser heals itself on the next tick instead of needing the store cleared.
